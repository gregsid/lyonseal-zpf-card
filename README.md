## What the walk says

The capitalised words below are the walk's names for parts of your system; each is glossed where it first appears. The Lyonseal doesn't hand anyone a discovery. It walks a system through its phases and says where the stress is and what to retune first, so the person who knows the system can find the next step faster. My AI chairs ran your v1.1 report through it twelve times, on two different models, each walk blind to the others. Twelve of twelve put the primary stress in the same place.

**Primary stress: the Coupling — the step from your structure to anything you can measure.** Your framework is now explicit enough to be killed, and that is new. But nothing yet passes through the interface: every prediction in §12 is conditional on a cosmic history K(τ) the report doesn't give, and every number that would tie the structure to a measurement (C⋆, A_g, E_Σ) is open. In the walk's words: a well-made shutter with nothing yet passing through it. The usual failure at this step is an interface that lets too much through; yours is the opposite. Your own word for it, from your Oct 25 post, is coupling.

**Secondary stress: the Substrate.** The plenum is a premise, Σ is a label, and the basis is one dimensional direction short (your §7.6: "One additional dimensional direction is necessary"). The report says so itself. The Crucible, your anti-target discipline and no-go ledger, is the strongest phase you have. Don't loosen it to reach the numbers.

## One clue from the walk

Four of your conditional predictions (51)–(55) hold for any history K(τ): constant α⋆, constant a⋆ and E_n, constant λ_emit, and D/c₀ = A_atomic as an identity. The fifth, (53), reads atomic and Compton clock rates against orbital time, and it has no size until there is a bound on K(τ). So §12.1 as it stands cannot tell your framework from the standard picture, and that sorts your test list for you: the invariances are consistency checks, not the kill conditions your §11.1 asks for.

Where this came from, exactly: the first walk of your report found it at the Coupling. The twelve blind walks all named the Coupling, but none of them stated this clue. Three other readings of the report, given no walk at all, stated it independently. So the walk gave the place; the checks gave the content.

## The numbers that back this (full workings in WORKINGS.md)

- **Actuator.** Walls moving 1 nm at 1 GHz reach about 6 m/s, 2×10⁻⁸ of light speed; the light in a 200-nm gap just follows them (adiabatically). Your η_b ≈ 0.08 came from Wilson's circuit (a few percent of light speed, pumped at twice the photon frequency); it needs a scaling law to carry over, and your Qualifying Paper's abstract already lists η_b among the "experimentally testable working hypotheses rather than universal constants".
- **Ledger.** Your home page's limit for radiated light, F ≤ P_rad/c, is 33 nN at 10 W. Your pre-seed page projects 3–15 mN. Name the momentum mechanism before any thrust stand. (The same page gives 0.6–9 N per cm² and 576–10,891 N for 100 cm²; 0.6–9 N/cm² × 100 cm² is 60–900 N, so one of those two lines is about ten times off.)
- **Bench.** Light guided along a 200-nm silver slot would see a vacuum change ΔK as a phase shift of about k₀·L·ΔK. Photon noise allows about 10⁻¹²; in practice the limit is how well the walls' own motion is calibrated out, about 10⁻⁷ in ΔK at best (WORKINGS §6: that needs the stroke known to 1 part in 5,000). Standard physics predicts zero along the gap, so the size has to come from your framework, stated in advance. A clean null below a line you stated beforehand is a result.
